Finding Accommodation

Where students live, what it costs, how to find a flat through people rather than listings, and what to check before you pay anything.

سكن
About This Guide

This guide is written specifically for students living and studying in Cairo — primarily Nasr City, where the vast majority of Western students settle. It does not cover other Egyptian cities or districts. A small number of students do end up studying elsewhere in Egypt, but this is uncommon, and we don't currently have reliable information to offer on those areas. Based on the first-hand experience of students on the ground, expanded and structured by Al-Maslak al-Muyassar. Your experience may vary — use this as a foundation and lean on the student community for the latest.

There is no central listing site for student housing in Egypt. Almost everything is found through people. Once you are connected to the student community, options become available quickly.

When to Start Looking

Ask roughly one month before you want to move in — not too far in advance. Things change constantly and a room you are told about today may be gone before you arrive.

Two Factors Students Consistently Underestimate

Noise Pollution
Near a main road: constant traffic, beeping, dogs, disturbance — especially at night. Cheaper flats tend to be noisier. Consider your sleep and study focus before choosing purely on price.
Floor Level
Many buildings have no working lift. Being on the 8th floor means climbing stairs five times a day for ṣalāh. Students report this becoming exhausting and making them reluctant to go out.

How to Find a Flat

  1. WhatsApp and Telegram student groupsThe most effective route. Ask to be added to housing groups — rooms come up regularly and are often already informally vetted.
  2. Ask at your institute on arrivalMany institutes have notice boards or staff who know of available flats.
  3. Harunco Real Estate Egypt (Telegram)A Telegram-based service students have used for finding accommodation. Link on the Community page.
  4. Facebook groupsSearch "Cairo housing," "Muslims in Cairo," "Egypt expats." Check post dates — listings go stale quickly.
  5. Local brokers (simsār)Charge roughly one month's rent as a fee (non-refundable). Only use one pressed for time, with a recommendation. Go directly to the landlord where possible — estate agents add cost and complexity.
Never Pay Without Viewing

Never send money for a flat before physically seeing it. Scams targeting foreign students exist. If you have no connections on arrival, pre-book an Airbnb in Nasr City for a few nights and view flats in person from there.

Before You Sign Anything

  • Get a written contract — essential protection against unexpected eviction
  • Pay a deposit (~one month's rent) — photograph the condition of everything on arrival
  • Simsār fee (~one month's rent) if using an agent — non-refundable
  • Unfurnished flats can be cheaper long-term but require buying all appliances including AC and stove
  • Is electricity included? Water? Internet? Who pays if bills are not included?
  • Is there a working lift? What floor is the flat on?
  • Who to contact if something breaks?
Tap Water

Do not drink the tap water in Cairo. Buy bottled water or install a filter. This is an ongoing cost most guides don't mention — budget for it from day one.

On bills

Many landlords include electricity, water and sometimes internet when renting to foreign students. Always confirm this before signing. Air conditioning can make summer electricity bills spike — clarify who pays if bills are not included.

Common problems — and how to avoid them

  1. Overpaying because you arrived with no local contactLandlords charge more when they can see you are new with no reference point. Before agreeing a price, ask another student what is normal for that area and flat size.
  2. Electricity bills jumping in summerIf bills are not included, budget extra from March onwards, and ask the landlord what an average monthly bill looks like.
  3. Landlords slow to fix thingsInspect thoroughly before committing: water pressure, every tap, electrical sockets and any appliances that are included.
  4. Contracts in Arabic onlyDo not sign anything you cannot read until someone you trust has translated it. A student who has been there longer, or your institute, may be able to help.

Questions to ask before you agree

  • Is electricity included? What about water and internet?
  • How much is the deposit, and when do I get it back?
  • Is there a written contract, and can I get a translated copy?
  • Who do I contact if something breaks?
  • Does the flat have a water filter, or will I buy bottled water?
  • Is there air conditioning, and who pays for the electricity it uses?
  • How far is the nearest institute?
  • Are other students living in the building?
  • Is there a washing machine, or a laundry nearby?

Your first week

Most experienced students book short-term accommodation for the first few days — even a cheap hotel in Nasr City — so they have a base while they look around in person. Committing to a flat from abroad without seeing it is a common mistake.

First-week priorities

Get a local SIM on arrival (Vodafone Egypt, Orange and Etisalat are the main providers), set up Uber or InDrive, and ask at your institute to be added to the student WhatsApp groups. Use a Starling or Revolut card, and avoid airport exchange bureaus — rates in Nasr City are much better.
