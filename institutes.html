Choosing Where to Study

Egypt offers a range of options depending on your level and goals. The main choice is between a structured markaz and private tuition — and many students combine both.

About This Guide

This guide is written specifically for students living and studying in Cairo — primarily Nasr City, where the vast majority of Western students settle. It does not cover other Egyptian cities or districts. A small number of students do end up studying elsewhere in Egypt, but this is uncommon, and we don't currently have reliable information to offer on those areas. Based on the first-hand experience of students on the ground, expanded and structured by Al-Maslak al-Muyassar. Your experience may vary — use this as a foundation and lean on the student community for the latest.

Quick picks

A starting point, not a verdict — read the full cards before you decide.

  • Complete beginner who wants to speak: Fajr, Raseel or Lisān al-ʿArab.
  • Serious, structured study: Al-Ibānah — it asks more time and effort of the student, and some prior experience is recommended. Its teachers stay on the same level for years.
  • Best value: Al-Fusha and Markaz Ḍiyā.
  • Pushing into higher levels: Markaz Furqān.
  • Sisters: Dār Ḥafṣah is a female-only institute; Al-Tibyan Academy offers Qurʾān study for women.
  • Memorising the Qurʾān: Al-Qurʾān Ḥayāti, Mahad Al-Hafidh, Nabūgh (brothers) and Al-Tibyan.

Islamic Sciences

Some students begin Islamic sciences alongside Arabic; others finish Arabic first. Both approaches are valid.

A Realistic Expectation

Following lessons in Arabic is very difficult at first. With time it becomes easier and actually assists your Arabic significantly. Be patient with yourself if you start early.

Finding Scholars and Specialised Teachers

This operates almost entirely through recommendations and word of mouth. Most respected scholars are not publicly advertised. You reach them through a connection — another student introduces you. Many have Telegram groups where they share syllabuses and schedules.

Mutūn

  • A teacher visits a student's home — open invitation or closed class for invited students
  • Lessons at the teacher's own location
  • Some marākiz offer mutūn study as part of a distinct academic progression
Serious Warning — Villages and Police

Police specifically patrol villages populated with Ahl al-Sunnah and actively look for reasons to arrest foreigners — sometimes with no legal justification beyond their presence in the area. If considering villages, seek advice from trusted brothers with experience first, and never go without full documentation. This is not a theoretical risk.
