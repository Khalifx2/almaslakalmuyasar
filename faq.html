Honest answers to the questions students ask most before they go.

سؤال
Markaz Ibānah requires more time and effort from the student, and some prior experience is recommended — Madinah Book 1 or Bayna Yadayk Level 1 is a good foundation. Speaking-focused marākiz like Fajr and Raseel are very comfortable for complete beginners. For daily life, you will mostly encounter Egyptian dialect, not fuṣḥā — be prepared for a learning curve in everyday communication regardless of your Arabic level.
For a meaningful foundation in Arabic, most students commit to at least 6–12 months. A single level (around 4–6 weeks at most institutes) covers very little on its own — the benefit compounds over many levels. Students pursuing both Arabic and Islamic sciences often stay 1–3 years. Come with a realistic plan and do not underestimate how long real progress takes.
For the most part, yes — particularly in student areas of Nasr City enrolled in established institutes. Main risks are: police stops targeting brothers with visible Islamic appearance in certain areas or villages; expired visa documentation; and standard urban risks. Stay in student areas, maintain valid documents, stay in the support groups, and you will be fine in most circumstances. Villages are significantly higher risk.
Yes — many students come with families. Budget for a larger, nicer apartment for safety and comfort, which increases costs significantly. Gardenia City Compound, Rehab City, or New Cairo/Tagammou are more family-suited. Plan for at least double the accommodation budget of a single student, plus additional family costs. The trade-off is distance from institutes and higher monthly spending.
Many students find a combination works best — a markaz for structure and accountability, a private teacher to cover gaps or advance faster in specific areas. Private tuition alone requires strong self-discipline; without formal exams, progress can drift without you noticing. Try both and calibrate to what benefits you most.
For Arabic: complete at least Madinah Book 1 or Bayna Yadayk Level 1 before arriving if you can — it helps you benefit from day one, especially once institutes move into deeper grammar in later levels. For Qurʾān: work on tajwīd issues with a local teacher first. For Islamic sciences: prior exposure to basic ʿAqeedah and Fiqh will help you follow lessons faster when you start.

Dār Ḥafṣah is a female-only Arabic institute in Nasr City, and sisters also study at institutes such as Fajr (female classes start 30 minutes after the male slots) and Markaz Ibānah (some gender-segregated provision, subject to availability).
